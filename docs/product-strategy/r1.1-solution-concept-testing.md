## Concept 1 — Knowledge-First Employee Self-Service & Catalog Administration *(Provisional Priority 1)*

- **Customer Problem:** Employees frequently submit repetitive IT, HR, and workplace requests because internal policies and how-to guides are scattered across wikis, PDFs, and chat threads—while administrators cannot easily maintain a clean, department-scoped service catalog without technical intervention.
- **Current Workaround:** Employees message IT/HR staff directly on Slack/Teams or email shared inboxes; admins maintain static Confluence/SharePoint pages disconnected from request forms.
- **Proposed Outcome:** A unified self-hosted portal where authorised administrators manage service catalog offerings directly, and employees searching for help see role-appropriate knowledge articles *before* filing a ticket, with a clear fallback to submit a structured service request when self-service does not answer their question.
- **Main Users:** Employees (requesters), HR & IT Specialists (knowledge authors), Admins (catalog managers).
- **Primary Buyer / Co-Sponsor:** Head of IT / VP Operations (buyer); HR Operations & Support Leads (co-sponsors).
- **Required Capabilities (Under Evaluation):** Admin catalog CRUD UI, knowledge article lifecycle (draft/review/publish/archive), version history, strict role/department visibility controls (preventing internal specialist notes from reaching general employees), search with contextual suggestions during ticket creation, helpful/not-helpful feedback, and OIDC SSO.
- **Possible Adoption Barriers (Actively Probe):**
  - *Who owns writing and keeping articles up to date?* (Stale KB kills adoption.)
  - *Existing investment in Confluence, Notion, or SharePoint:* Will teams refuse to maintain a second knowledge repository instead of linking to existing docs?
  - *Missing file attachments or rich formatting.*

## Concept 2 — Configurable Cross-Department Workflow Automation *(Provisional Priority 2)*

- **Customer Problem:** When internal approval policies, spend thresholds, or department routing rules change, teams are forced either to chase manual approvals over email/spreadsheets, pay for expensive SaaS Enterprise tiers / metered automation transactions, or maintain brittle user-owned scripts that break when staff leave.
- **Current Workaround:** Email approval chains, Power Automate flows subject to daily limits and owner-account fragility, manual IT ticket reassignments, or fixed hard-coded rules.
- **Proposed Outcome:** A declarative, server-validated workflow configuration workspace where authorised administrators define multi-step approval and routing workflows using safe, allowlisted conditions and actions—and **simulate** synthetic scenarios before publishing—with version-pinned active runs and an auditable execution trail on self-hosted infrastructure.
- **Main Users:** Systems Administrators, HR Ops Leads, IT Service Managers (configurers); Department Managers & Specialists (task participants); Auditors (reviewers).
- **Primary Buyer / Co-Sponsor:** Head of IT / Systems Administrator (buyer); InfoSec & Operations Leaders (co-sponsors).
- **Required Capabilities (Under Evaluation):** Visual/declarative workflow editor, conditional branching (e.g., department, priority, estimated cost), multi-stage approvals, automated escalations/timeouts, dry-run simulation showing expected path & dead ends, version history & rollback, strict allowlisted actions (no arbitrary user scripts), and durable crash-recoverable execution logs.
- **Possible Adoption Barriers (Actively Probe):**
  - *Configuration complexity:* Is a visual workflow builder actually usable by HR Ops, or will it still bottleneck on IT admins?
  - *External system triggers:* Do workflows fail to deliver value unless they directly call external APIs (Active Directory, Okta, Google Workspace, Jira, Payroll)?
  - *Governance fear:* Risk of misconfigured workflows looping or stalling urgent requests.

## Concept 3 — Employee Lifecycle Orchestration *(Provisional Priority 3)*

- **Customer Problem:** Major employee lifecycle transitions—onboarding, department transfers, and offboarding—span HR, IT, Facilities, Finance, and Managers. Tracking these handoffs via spreadsheets and disconnected tickets leads to unprepared Day-1 new hires and high-risk delays in revoking departing employees' access.
- **Current Workaround:** Shared Excel/Google Sheets checklists, manual email chains, disconnected IT tickets, and post-hoc security audit reconciliations.
- **Proposed Outcome:** A coordinated multi-department journey tracker initiated by HR (or authorised workflow trigger) that spawns sequenced/dependent tasks across IT, Facilities, Finance, and Managers, surfaces blockers and completion progress in one view, enforces approval gates before sensitive actions (such as access revocation), and records an auditable trail while keeping sensitive HR data isolated.
- **Main Users:** HR Operations (initiators/coordinators), IT/Facilities/Finance Specialists (task owners), Hiring/Department Managers, InfoSec/Auditors.
- **Primary Buyer / Co-Sponsor:** HR Operations Lead & InfoSec Lead (champions); Head of IT / COO (buyers).
- **Required Capabilities (Under Evaluation):** Onboarding, transfer, and offboarding journey templates, cross-department task dependencies and deadlines, completion evidence, blocker visibility, strict HR field-level PII isolation, and mandatory approval gating before any account/access deactivation.
- **Possible Adoption Barriers (Actively Probe):**
  - *HRIS overlap:* Does the organisation's existing HRIS (e.g., Zoho People, BambooHR, Workday, Keka) already provide "good enough" onboarding checklists?
  - *Automated provisioning expectations:* Will IT reject an orchestrator that creates auditable human tasks unless it also automates Entra ID / Google Workspace account creation & revocation?
  - *Low event volume:* In a `150`-person company hiring `2` people/month, is onboarding frequent enough to justify adopting a dedicated platform?
